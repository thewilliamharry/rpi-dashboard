<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| worker entrypoint → scheduler/jobs | Lifecycle ordering determines when network, browser, and persistence work becomes active. |
| scheduler threads → SQLite repositories | Concurrent job lanes share the database through explicit short transactions. |
| preview job → Chromium/network | Optional browser work crosses a high-resource and outbound-network boundary. |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-01-11 | Tampering / Denial of service | worker startup ordering | high | mitigate | Tests require database readiness before heartbeat, scheduler, signals, browser, or jobs. |
| T-01-12 | Denial of service | scheduler composition | medium | mitigate | Preserve bounded executors, coalescing, `max_instances=1`, and misfire grace per named job. |
| T-01-13 | Elevation of privilege | domain import direction | medium | mitigate | Flask-free modules receive dependencies explicitly and cannot reach composition globals. |
| T-01-14 | Denial of service | Playwright lifecycle | medium | mitigate | Lazy worker-only construction, serialized ownership, deadline budgets, and `finally` cleanup. |
| T-01-15 | Repudiation | transition/alert persistence | low | accept | Existing event/alert outcome rows remain the local audit evidence; no multi-user attribution is in project scope. |
</threat_model>
