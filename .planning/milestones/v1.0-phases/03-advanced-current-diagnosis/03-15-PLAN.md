<objective>
Make a genuine background-job failure survive a bookkeeping failure, without reopening the false positive 03-12 closed.

Purpose: 03-12 correctly stopped a failing `succeeded` write from being durably recorded as a work failure. The same edit moved the work's exception into a local variable, which removed Python's implicit chaining, and then raised the bookkeeping condition `from` the *write* error. The verifier reproduced the consequence: when the work raises and the outcome write then also fails, the genuine failure is gone from every channel at once -- the durable row stays running, no operator exception is composed, the raised condition names the write error's class, both `__cause__` and `__context__` are the write error, and nothing logs the work error. Before 03-12 the work error survived in `__context__` and reached the container log through APScheduler's `exc_info=True`. It no longer does. This is the phase's own standing prohibition against suppressing a genuine failure while narrowing a false one, and it flipped from upheld to violated this round.

Two errors are real on that path and both must be reported. The bookkeeping failure keeps its own identity, as 03-12 established; the work failure regains a channel -- three of them, in fact: the condition carries the work's bounded class, the chain binds the work error, and the log states it. Separately, the durable row left in the running state must stop being invisible: a job whose outcome was never recorded and which is now overdue against its own cadence becomes an actionable exception on the surface an operator actually looks at, derived from that job's own row.

Output: A dispatch path where separating two failures costs neither of them its visibility, an operator surface that shows a job stuck without an outcome, and a startup path that no longer dies on a transient lock.
</objective>
