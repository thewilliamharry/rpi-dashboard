<objective>
Close the third defect behind G-03.1-2. `connect_db` hands every ordinary connection a shared
`flock` on the maintenance lock and holds it for that connection's lifetime, released only by
`close()`. In `dashboard/app.py`, connections are opened by assignment and closed by a bare
statement at the end of the block, so any exception between the two skips the close and the lease
survives on the garbage collector's schedule. Twenty-two of twenty-seven sites share that shape as a
pre-existing latent condition; phase 03.1 made one of them raise reliably, because
`read_maintenance_windows_by_port` queries a table that does not exist on a schema still at version
8. On the Pi that turned every dashboard poll into a leaked lease, and leaked leases are precisely
what starved the worker's exclusive acquisition.

The fix is the one the gap names: reuse the `database_access` context manager that already exists in
`dashboard/beacon/db.py` rather than inventing a boundary. Most of the change is mechanical, but it
is not uniformly so, and the exceptions are where the risk lives. Measured against the current file:

- Twenty-four sites are a `with _db_lock:` statement immediately followed by the connection
  assignment. Merging the two context managers into one `with` statement leaves the body at its
  existing indentation at twenty-three of them.
- Of the file's thirty-four connection-close statements, thirty sit inside those twenty-four blocks,
  one is in `init_db`, two are in the runtime-state helpers, and one is in `_worker_write_transaction`
  — which opens through `connect_db` rather than the seam, already owns its close in a `finally`
  suite, and is out of scope here.
- Every close inside the twenty-four blocks and `init_db` is either its block's last statement or
  immediately precedes a `return`, so deleting it never extends a lease across later work.
- But four of those closes are the sole statement of a `finally:` suite, and deleting a suite's only
  statement is a syntax error, not a no-op. Two of the four (`_mutation_write_transaction` and the
  preview-publish block) also carry an `except` clause, so only the `finally:` clause is removed. Two
  (`init_db` and the telemetry-history block) have no handler at all, so the whole `try:` scaffold
  goes and their bodies genuinely do dedent — the only two places in this change where a body line
  moves.
- And `_get_runtime_state` is the one site whose close is followed by real work: it closes, then
  decodes the stored JSON. Its conversion must end the connection scope before that decode, or the
  fix would itself introduce the lease extension this plan exists to prevent.

Those five facts, not a blanket "mechanical", are what makes this safe to execute.

Purpose: stop the web process from contributing to the starvation that took the dashboard down, and
remove the whole defect class rather than the one site phase 03.1 lit up.

Output: context-managed ownership at every connection site, an end-to-end regression proving a
failing request leaves the migrator able to proceed, and a structural gate that keeps the class
closed.
</objective>
