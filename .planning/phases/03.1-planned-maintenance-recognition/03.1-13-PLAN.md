<objective>
Close the third defect behind G-03.1-2. `connect_db` hands every ordinary connection a shared
`flock` on the maintenance lock and holds it for that connection's lifetime, released only by
`close()`. In `dashboard/app.py`, connections are opened by assignment and closed by a bare
statement at the end of the block, so any exception between the two skips the close and the lease
survives on the garbage collector's schedule. Twenty-two of twenty-seven sites share that shape as a
pre-existing latent condition; phase 03.1 made one of them raise reliably, because
`read_maintenance_windows_by_port` queries a table that does not exist on a schema still at version
8. On the Pi that turned every dashboard poll into a leaked lease, and leaked leases are precisely
what starved the worker's exclusive acquisition.

The fix is the one the gap names: reuse the `database_access` context manager that already exists in
`dashboard/beacon/db.py` rather than inventing a boundary. The change is unusually mechanical, and
two measurements make it low risk. Twenty-four of the sites are a `with _db_lock:` statement
immediately followed by the connection assignment, so merging the two context managers into one
`with` statement leaves every body line at its existing indentation. And every one of the thirty-one
connection-close statements inside those blocks is either the block's last statement or immediately
precedes a `return` — so deleting them and letting the context manager close never extends a lease
across work that used to run after the close.

Purpose: stop the web process from contributing to the starvation that took the dashboard down, and
remove the whole defect class rather than the one site phase 03.1 lit up.

Output: context-managed ownership at every connection site, an end-to-end regression proving a
failing request leaves the migrator able to proceed, and a structural gate that keeps the class
closed.
</objective>
