<objective>
`_ATTRIBUTION_MAX_ANCHOR_STEPS = 64` (`dashboard/beacon/maintenance.py:319`) bounds boundary
discovery at 129 days, while the constant's own comment claims "64 steps covers well over a year".
The arithmetic behind the claim: each step after the first adds
`MAINTENANCE_OCCURRENCE_LOOKBACK_DAYS` new calendar days, so `N` steps cover `2N + 1` days — 129 at
`N = 64`, and 365 days would need 183. Past the bound, `_covering_boundaries` simply stops yielding,
so the older portion of a long offline interval is never split at its coverage boundaries and its
maintenance-attributed seconds are silently dropped. Measured during planning against a daily
60-minute window with the ceiling lifted: 150 days attributes 150.0 hours and 400 days attributes
400.0 hours, where the shipped bound returns 129.0 hours for both (the shipped reach is 2N+1 = 129 days, not N x L = 128; truncation begins at 130 days, and 128- and 129-day intervals still attribute exactly).

**The exposure is via a different caller than the code review named, and this plan states the
correction rather than inheriting the error.** 03.1-REVIEW.md attributed the exposure to
`/api/services` and treated its 8-day bound as the mitigation. That bound is real —
`CHECK_RETENTION_SECONDS` is a fixed `UPTIME_WINDOW_SECONDS + 86400`, NOT derived from `EXPIRE_DAYS`
— but it is the wrong caller. The reachable path is `dashboard/beacon/diagnosis.py:637`
`get_current_diagnosis`, serving `/api/advanced/current`, which sets
`period_seconds = settings.expire_days * 86400` and passes it straight in. `_positive_int` enforces
no upper bound, so `EXPIRE_DAYS=400` is accepted. The default deployment at `EXPIRE_DAYS=7` is
unaffected, which is why this is Minor and not more — but PROJECT.md's own constraint puts advanced
analytics at a rolling 90 days, so the configured value an operator would reasonably choose already
sits within a factor of one and a half of a bound whose comment tells them they have a year.

**The shape of the fix.** The step counter was only ever an anti-runaway guard; the loop's real
termination is its own `anchor >= interval_start - 86400` condition. So the budget becomes derived
from the interval boundary discovery must actually cover, and the fixed number becomes an absolute
ceiling that no interval a configured deployment can produce will ever reach — honest about its true
reach, and, when it does bind on a nonsensical interval, saying so rather than returning a short
number. Deriving is chosen over capping `EXPIRE_DAYS` deliberately: clamping an operator's configured
retention would be a second silent truncation one layer down, and the gap's failure mode is silence,
not size.

This plan also carries the round's closure record. Three gaps were opened together and are closed
together, and each is written so the next verifier runs a check rather than re-reads code.

Purpose: eliminate silent under-counting of maintenance-attributed downtime at any retention the
deployment is configured for, and leave this round auditable rather than asserted.

Output: a derived, honest, non-silent anchor bound with a long-interval regression that fails at the
shipped number, a measured cost ceiling that keeps the fix servable on a Pi, and a UAT record
answering all three gaps item by item.
</objective>
